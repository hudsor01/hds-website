## The Anatomy of a Compliant Paystub

Texas does not mandate a specific paystub format, but the Texas Payday Law does require an earnings statement with every payment. It has to show the employee's name, rate of pay, total pay earned for the period, each deduction and its purpose, net pay and hours worked if you pay by the hour. Federal law does not require a paystub at all. The Fair Labor Standards Act does require accurate payroll records, though. The paystub is how an employee sees them. When you hire a developer in Plano or a crew lead in Fort Worth, that document proves you are paying correctly. Missing one line item invites disputes. Employees who cannot verify their pay stop trusting the rest of what you tell them.

A proper payroll record tracks four distinct buckets.
- Earnings: base salary, hourly wages, overtime and bonuses. Each type needs its own line with hours worked and rate applied.
- Pre-tax deductions: health insurance premiums, 401k contributions and dependent care accounts. These lower the employee's taxable income. Section 125 items like health premiums and dependent care also cut Social Security and Medicare tax for both of you, while traditional 401k deferrals lower income tax withholding but not FICA.
- Post-tax deductions: wage garnishments, union fees and after-tax retirement contributions. These do not reduce taxable wages, so keep them separate from pre-tax items.
- Employer contributions: state unemployment tax, federal unemployment tax and your matching share of Social Security and Medicare. Workers compensation premiums belong on your books too. Most employers leave these off the employee paystub, but you absolutely must track them for quarterly filings.

The processor calculates gross pay, applies the 7.65 percent employee FICA rate (6.2 percent Social Security up to the annual wage base plus 1.45 percent Medicare) and routes the final numbers to accounting. Nobody retypes hours. The paystub becomes an automated output instead of a manual spreadsheet nightmare. ## Why Manual Paystubs Drain Your Margins

Revenue operations taught me what happens when a business runs on fragments instead of integrated systems. You drop a timesheet into Excel, copy paste it to QuickBooks and hope the math holds up. Eventually it does not. One misplaced decimal or a wrong W-4 setup can understate what you owe the IRS for a whole quarter. The penalties and interest show up after the money is already spent.

Manual paystubs create invisible drag on your operations. You spend time formatting PDFs instead of closing deals. Your bookkeeper chases missing W-4 forms from new hires. Employees ask basic questions about net pay because the numbers look wrong. Every payroll error costs you twice: once in the hours spent finding and correcting it, again in the reissued documents and the trust you burned with the employee.

Pick a target payroll error rate, such as under half a percent of paystubs. Track it every cycle. Once you hold that line, the time you used to spend on damage control goes back into growth.

## The DFW Compliance Landscape You Cannot Ignore

Texas has no state income tax, so a Texas paystub has no state withholding line. That does not mean there are no state rules. The Texas Payday Law requires most nonexempt employees to be paid at least twice a month and exempt employees at least once a month. You also file quarterly wage reports and pay state unemployment tax through the Texas Workforce Commission. If you run a software shop in Frisco and hire a remote developer in another state, that state's withholding and paystub rules apply to that employee. Check the rules every time you hire outside Texas because a missed filing triggers penalties. When December comes around, [the payroll and contractor paperwork to gather before the year closes](/blog/year-end-checklist-for-small-business-owners) builds on these same records.

Federal requirements stay constant. You must display the required federal labor posters, keep payroll records for at least three years and keep time cards and wage rate tables for at least two. The IRS separately expects employment tax records kept for at least four years. Build a compliance checklist that covers minimum wage, tip credit rules and overtime. Minimum wage follows where the employee works, not where your clients are. Texas follows the federal minimum, so a remote employee in another state may be owed that state's higher rate. Tip credit is the payroll half of tipping for a restaurant. The table half is [splitting the check and the tip at checkout](/blog/tip-calculation-and-bill-splitting), which slows turnover when servers do it by hand.

Measuring compliance is about tracking three leading indicators.
- Record retention: every timesheet, paystub and tax filing stored in one place with timestamps. A Power BI report on top of that archive can pull up the audit trail in seconds during an inspection.
- Deduction accuracy: pre-tax and post-tax splits checked against current IRS withholding tables. Run a monthly reconciliation that flags any variance above two percent before the quarter closes.
- Employee self-service: a simple portal where staff can download their own paystubs without emailing you. Most payroll platforms include one. Turn it on so HR stops acting as a file courier.

When you treat the paystub as a living compliance asset instead of a static PDF, audits become routine. Complete, timestamped documentation is the whole defense. The system does the heavy lifting while you focus on scaling operations.

## Measure What Matters and Fix the Bleed

I run everything by metrics because unmeasured processes stay broken forever. Your payroll dashboard should track five core numbers each pay cycle.
- Gross to net ratio: each employee's net pay as a share of gross. A sudden swing from one cycle to the next signals incorrect withholding or a missing benefit deduction.
- Overtime frequency: how many employees hit overtime each week. Pick a ceiling, say fifteen percent of total hours, that triggers schedule adjustments or a hiring review.
- Deduction error rate: mismatches between offer terms and actual payouts. Keep this below one percent by cross-referencing employee profiles monthly.
- Pay distribution speed: time from approved timesheets to final paystub generation. Automated routing gets this down to hours instead of days.
- Employee retrieval rate: percentage of staff who download their own paystubs from the portal. Every self-serve download is one less HR email and one less duplicate request.

Connect these metrics to a Power BI dashboard that refreshes every Friday afternoon. Green, yellow and red indicators show the health of each pay cycle. Review the board with whoever runs operations before processing payroll. If overtime frequency spikes, adjust shift assignments immediately. If deduction error rate climbs, audit W-4 forms and benefit elections. The system catches problems before they hit the bank account. You stop reacting to payroll disasters and start optimizing labor costs with real data.

The paystub becomes a diagnostic tool instead of an administrative chore. Track it alongside revenue metrics because labor expense directly impacts gross margin. When you align payroll accuracy with sales forecasting, you protect profit margins and build trust with your team. Clean documents delivered on time remove one more reason for good people to leave.
