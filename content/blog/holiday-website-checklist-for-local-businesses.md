Picture a shop owner in Plano on December 26. The phone rang all weekend. Half the callers were annoyed and the other half asked questions the website should have answered. The site said they were open Christmas Eve. They weren't. Google said they closed at 5. They closed at 3. This holiday website checklist exists so that's never you. It takes an afternoon. Do it this week, before Thanksgiving traffic shows up and small mistakes start costing you real customers.

I spent nearly a decade in revenue operations before I built websites, so I treat the holidays like quarter end. Broken handoffs hurt most when the people looking for you are ready to buy today. Here's what to check, in the order I'd check it.

## 1. Holiday Hours Everywhere, Not Just Somewhere

Wrong hours are the easiest holiday mistake to make. They're also the most expensive, because they burn trust with people who already decided to show up.

Write down your real schedule first. Thanksgiving, the Friday after, Small Business Saturday, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, New Year's Eve and New Year's Day. Include early closes. "Open" with a 2 p.m. close is a different answer than "open."

Then put that schedule in every place a customer might see it.

### Google Business Profile special hours

Your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist) has a dedicated special hours setting for holidays. Use it. Don't edit your regular hours and plan to change them back in January, because you'll forget and you'll be wrong for months. Special hours override your normal schedule for just those dates, then fall away on their own.

Plenty of people searching "near me" in Frisco or Arlington never click through to your site. They read your hours right off the map listing. If that listing is wrong, a perfect website won't save the visit. If your profile isn't showing up at all, fix that first with [this walkthrough](/blog/why-google-business-profile-not-showing-up).

### Your website, in three spots

On the site itself, holiday hours belong in three places:

- A slim banner across the top of every page, with the dates in plain words
- Your contact page, right next to the phone number
- The footer, if that's where your regular hours live

Keep the wording identical across all of them and across Google. If your banner says "Closed Dec 24 to 26" and Google says you're open the 26th, customers trust neither.

Also check Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook and any booking platform you use. Apple Maps is the built-in map app on every iPhone, yet plenty of owners have never claimed their listing there.

## 2. Put the Seasonal Offer on the Homepage

If you're running a holiday promotion and it only lives on Instagram, most of your site visitors will never see it.

The offer goes above the fold on your homepage. One headline, one line of detail, one button. "Book a December detail by the 15th and get the second car half off" beats a carousel of five rotating slides every time. Rotating sliders hide your best message behind a timer. On a phone, plenty of people scroll past before slide two ever shows up.

A few rules I follow:

- **Put an end date on it.** A real deadline gives people a reason to act now instead of "after the holidays."
- **Link to one page.** The button should go to a page about that offer, not your general services page. If you want a refresher on what that page needs, my post on [landing page optimization](/blog/landing-page-optimization-small-business) covers it.
- **Match the offer to your capacity.** If you can only take 40 bookings in December, say so. Scarcity that's true converts. Scarcity that's fake gets noticed.

And when the promo ends, pull it down the same day. A homepage still advertising Black Friday pricing on January 9 tells visitors nobody's minding the store.

## 3. Make Gift Cards and Booking Easy to Find

For salons, restaurants, med spas, fitness studios and auto services, gift cards can be a big December revenue line. They're also exactly what people buy at 10 p.m. on December 22 from the couch.

If buying a gift card on your site means calling the front desk during business hours, you're losing that sale to whoever made it easier.

### What a good gift card path looks like

- A "Gift Cards" link in the main navigation from Thanksgiving through New Year's, not buried in the footer
- A purchase flow that works on a phone without pinching or zooming
- An instant email delivery option for the last-minute buyer
- A clear line about how the recipient redeems it

If you already take payments through Square, it sells eGift cards you can link straight from your site. On Stripe or another processor it takes a little more setup. Either way, this is the season it pays for itself.

### Booking for the January rush

Holiday shoppers are also planning. Someone who buys a massage gift card in December is booking that massage in January. If your booking calendar only shows two weeks out, open it further. And if your no-show rate climbs every January, my post on [online booking that cuts no-shows](/blog/online-booking-that-cuts-no-shows) walks through the reminder setup that fixes most of it.

## 4. Speed Test Before the Traffic Spike

A slow site on a normal Tuesday is annoying. A slow site on the Saturday after Thanksgiving, when people are comparing three shops on their phone in a parking lot at Stonebriar, is lost money.

Run your homepage and your offer page through Google's PageSpeed Insights on the mobile setting. Look at Largest Contentful Paint first. That's roughly how long the main content takes to show up, which is what customers actually feel. If you want to understand the other scores, [Core Web Vitals explained](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained-for-small-business) breaks them down in plain terms.

The usual holiday culprits:

- **A giant hero image** someone swapped in for the season, uploaded straight from a camera at several megabytes
- **A new popup or chat widget** added for the promo that loads a pile of third-party scripts
- **A video background** that looked great on the designer's desktop and crawls on LTE

Fix the image first. It's almost always the image.

If you want to put a dollar figure on what slow pages cost you, plug your numbers into my free [performance calculator](/tools/performance-calculator). It's a fast way to decide whether a speed fix is worth paying for before your busiest month.

## 5. Test Every Form and Every Phone Number

This is the step everyone skips. I never skip it. Forms break quietly. A plugin updates, an email password changes, a spam filter gets stricter. Suddenly your contact form "works" for the visitor but sends nothing to you.

Block out 20 minutes and do it yourself, on your own phone, on cell data rather than your office Wi-Fi.

### The form test

- Submit every form on the site with a real email you can check
- Confirm the notification lands in the inbox your team actually watches during the holidays
- Confirm the visitor gets an auto-reply that sets expectations, like "We're closed Dec 24 to 26 and will reply by the 27th"
- If the form feeds a CRM like HubSpot or Salesforce, check that the record showed up with the right source

If your forms ask for more than they need, trim them while you're in there. [Lead capture forms that convert](/blog/lead-capture-forms-that-convert) covers what to cut.

### The phone test

Tap every phone number on the site from a mobile browser. Each one should open the dialer with the right number. Then call it during a time you'll be closed. What does the caller hear?

Record a short seasonal greeting with your holiday hours and the fastest way to reach you, whether that's the booking link or a text line. If you forward emergency calls to a cell, test that too. HVAC and plumbing shops in North Texas know a hard freeze can hit any week from December into February.

## 6. Plan the Follow-Up Before the Holidays Start

This is where the RevOps habit kicks in. The holidays create a pile of new contacts: gift card buyers, first-time visitors, people who filled out a form on December 23 and heard nothing for a week. Most local businesses let that list sit until it goes cold.

Build the follow-up now, while you have time to think.

### Who to follow up with

- **Gift card buyers.** A thank-you in early January, plus a small offer for their own next visit.
- **Gift card recipients.** When they redeem, they're a new customer. Capture their contact info at checkout and treat them like one.
- **Holiday form leads.** Anyone who reached out while you were closed gets a personal reply the first day you're back. Not a template. A real reply.
- **Happy December customers.** Ask for a Google review while the visit is fresh. My guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) has the exact timing and wording.

### Automate the boring part

You don't need an enterprise stack. A simple sequence in your email tool or CRM can send the thank-you, the review request and a January reminder on schedule. I've built these in HubSpot and Workato. The principle holds at any size: decide the steps once and let the system run them. For a starting point, [this post on automated follow-up](/blog/automate-customer-follow-up-email-sms) lays out a sequence that fits most service businesses.

Then put a reminder on your calendar for January 2 to take down the holiday banner, reset any hours you changed by hand and pull the expired offer off the homepage.

## Your Holiday Website Checklist, Condensed

Print this or paste it into your notes app:

1. Holiday hours set as special hours in Google Business Profile
2. Same hours on the site banner, contact page and footer
3. Apple Maps, Yelp, Facebook and booking tools updated
4. One clear seasonal offer above the fold, with an end date
5. Gift card link in the main nav and a mobile-friendly purchase flow
6. Booking calendar opened far enough into January
7. Mobile PageSpeed check on the homepage and offer page
8. Every form submitted and every notification confirmed
9. Every phone number tapped and the voicemail greeting updated
10. Follow-up sequence built for buyers, recipients, leads and reviews
11. January 2 reminder to clean it all up

None of this is hard. It just has to happen before the busy weeks.
