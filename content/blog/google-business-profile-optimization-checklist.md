Twenty two percent of those clicks call you directly. That is about forty calls a month. You close thirty five percent of them at a four thousand dollar average ticket. ## The Setup Phase: Foundation and Accuracy

### Business Name, Categories and Service Areas

Your profile name must match the name you use in the real world: on your signage, your website and your invoices. Do not stuff keywords into the business name. Extra keywords in the name field break Google's guidelines and can get your listing suspended. Picture a landscaping firm in Keller that adds "best lawn care Dallas" to its name field. One competitor report later the listing is down, the map pack spot is gone and the owner is stuck in reverification instead of answering calls.

Categories are where you actually win or lose. Pick one primary category that matches your core revenue driver, then add the secondary categories from Google's list that describe the rest of your work. A DFW HVAC contractor might lead with HVAC contractor, then add air conditioning repair service, furnace repair service and heating contractor. The primary category carries the most weight in map pack placement. Picking the wrong one is among the [common reasons a profile stops showing up in local search](/blog/why-google-business-profile-not-showing-up). Specific jobs that have no category of their own, like thermostat installation or commercial maintenance plans, belong in the services list with a short description of each.

Service areas should match where you actually take jobs, not every city in the metroplex. Google lets a service area business list up to twenty cities, zip codes or regions. A contractor in Grapevine might list Grapevine, Southlake, Colleyville, Euless and the handful of zip codes around them, then keep that list in step with the territories in the CRM. Your service area does not guarantee where you show up, but listing far north DFW suburbs where travel time kills your margin only invites calls you will turn down.

### Photos, Hours and Attributes That Actually Move Metrics

Visual assets drive conversion rate more than most owners realize. Google has said for years that listings with photos get more direction requests and more website clicks than listings without them. Start with six high resolution images covering your team, your equipment and completed jobsites. Picture a dental clinic in McKinney that swaps generic stock shots for real photos of its operatory and front desk staff. A nervous patient sees the room and the people before booking. People buy from humans and recognizable environments.

Hours and attributes require the same operational rigor as your website uptime SLA. Set special hours for every holiday before it arrives and update regular hours the day your schedule changes. A mismatched holiday hour sends callers to a locked door on a high value service day. Attributes like women owned, wheelchair accessible and free wifi signal trust and can tip a click your way in competitive markets. I map these attributes to your CRM contact record so sales reps know what to highlight during the first touch.

## The Maintenance Phase: Signals That Feed the System

### Reviews, Questions and Post Cadence

Review velocity matters as much as raw star count. Track the ratio of new reviews to monthly search volume so you can see when your pace slips. Picture an auto repair shop in Irving that earns two new five star reviews every week. A competitor with a slightly higher average and no review since spring looks stale next to it. Fresh social proof and consistent engagement read as a business that is open and busy right now. I build automated review requests in Workato that trigger forty eight hours after service completion and go to every customer, not only the happy ones. Google's policy bars review gating, where you screen customers and ask only the satisfied ones to post. When a negative review does land, an alert hits a dedicated Slack channel so a manager can respond the same day and fix the problem behind it.

Google has retired the old Questions and Answers section on Business Profiles. In its place, an AI feature in Google Maps answers shopper questions using your profile details, your website and your reviews. That makes the source material your new conversion copy. Take the exact objections your sales team hears daily (price ranges, warranty terms, service guarantees and response time) and answer them in your business description, your services list and an FAQ page on your website. Clear answers in those places reduce friction before a prospect ever calls and give Google accurate material to quote.

Google posts require a structured cadence that aligns with your inventory or appointment capacity. I schedule at least one post a month, rotating between service spotlights, limited availability windows and seasonal promotions. Each post includes a clear call to action that routes directly to your booking page or phone number. I track post engagement through UTM parameters and dashboard views in Power BI to measure actual impact on form submissions.

### Calls and Booking Automation

Google shut down Business Profile chat in July 2024, so your listing no longer takes messages. Every lead now leaves through one of three doors: the call button, the website link or the booking link. Make each one count. Put a call tracking number in the primary phone field and list your main line as an additional number, which Google's guidelines allow, so you keep your NAP consistent and still know which calls came from the profile. Add UTM parameters to the website link so those visits show up as their own source. Every lead that comes through those doors flows through Workato into HubSpot as a new marketing qualified lead with tagged source attributes. Your sales team sees the exact service request before dialing in.

Booking integration eliminates the back and forth that kills conversion rates. I point the appointment link on your profile to a scheduling tool that syncs with your team calendar and sends automated confirmation texts. Automated confirmations and reminders are one of the cheapest ways to cut no shows. You track booking completion rate alongside profile click through rate to measure the full funnel health.

## The Measurement Phase: Tracking What Matters

You cannot optimize what you do not measure. I build attribution dashboards in Power BI that pull profile data from Google's Business Profile Performance API and merge it with your CRM pipeline stages. That API replaced the old Google My Business API. The dashboard tracks impressions, website clicks, direction requests and call clicks. I weigh clicks and calls higher than impressions because they represent active intent. Phone calls come in on the tracking number, which routes to your main line or specific sales reps.

Conversion rate optimization starts at the destination click. I audit the landing page that receives profile traffic and ensure it matches the exact search intent. A DFW plumbing company in Arlington running emergency leak repair searches must land on a page with immediate phone number, service area confirmation and one click booking button. If you send that traffic to a generic homepage, you make an urgent caller hunt for your number. Some of them hit the back button before they even read your headline. I track bounce rate and time on page for profile landing pages alongside standard website traffic to identify friction points.

Attribution modeling reveals which local signals drive actual revenue. I map review velocity, post cadence and photo upload frequency to closed won deals in Salesforce. That analysis tells you which activity lines up with closed deals and which is busywork. My bet is that active profiles earn more trust than dormant ones, but your own pipeline data is the only proof that counts.
